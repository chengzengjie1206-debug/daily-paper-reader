## 摘要
全球海表温度（SST）主导着海气耦合和全球气候变率，其准确重建是气候监测和预测的基础。现有的 SST 重建产品主要提供由异构卫星数据和原位观测推导出的单一确定性场，这限制了它们表征观测不确定性和支持概率预测的能力。在此，我们介绍了“卫星与原位自适应引导估计”（SAGE），这是一个基于扩散的、具有不确定性感知能力的生成框架，用于概率性 SST 重建。SAGE 学习了历史 SST 变率的物理一致先验，并以多源观测为条件进行后验采样。通过渐进式数据融合策略，来自两颗风云三号 D 星（FengYun-3D）极轨卫星的观测数据约束了海盆尺度的结构，并利用稀疏的原位测量数据来细化局部异常和极端情况。由此产生的集合 SST 场很好地捕捉了观测不确定性和尺度相关的变率。针对独立原位观测的验证表明，与广泛使用的业务化产品相比，SAGE 显著降低了重建误差。当用于初始化预报系统时，SAGE 生成的 SST 场相对于当前的业务化分析场，显著降低了 10 天 SST 预报误差。在气候尺度上，与传统方法相比，由 SAGE 驱动的 2023-2024 年厄尔尼诺事件预报在捕捉其发生和强度演变方面显示出附加价值。我们的结果表明，SAGE 代表了迈向海洋状态估计和气候预测新范式的一步。

## Abstract
Accurate reconstruction of global Sea surface temperature (SST), which dominates the air-sea coupling and global climate variability, underpins climate monitoring and prediction. Existing SST reconstruction products primarily provide one deterministic field derived from heterogeneous satellite data and in situ observations, limiting their ability to represent observation uncertainty and to support probabilistic forecasting. Here, we introduce Satellite and in situ Adaptive Guided Estimation (SAGE), a diffusion-based uncertainty-aware generative framework for probabilistic SST reconstruction. SAGE learns a physically consistent prior of historical SST variability and performs posterior sampling conditioned on multi-source observations. Through a progressive data-fusion strategy, observations from two FengYun-3D polar-orbiting satellites constrain basin-scale structures and uses sparse in situ measurements to refine local anomalies and extremes. The resulting ensemble SST fields well capture observational uncertainty and scale-dependent variability. Validation against independent in situ observations shows that SAGE substantially reduces reconstruction errors compared with widely used operational products. When used to initialize forecasting systems, SAGE-generated SST fields substantially reduce 10-day SST forecast errors relative to current operational analyses. At the climate scale, SAGE-driven forecasts of the 2023-2024 El Nino event show added value in capturing its onset and intensity evolution compared to conventional approaches. Our results demonstrate that SAGE represents a step toward a new paradigm for ocean state estimation and climate prediction.
