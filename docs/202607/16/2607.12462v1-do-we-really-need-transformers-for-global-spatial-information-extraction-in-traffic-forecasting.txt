Abstract 

Existing traffic forecasting models commonly focus on ex-

tracting spatial dependencies, particularly global spatial in-

formation, which characterizes the representations obtained 

through interactions between each individual node and all 

nodes across the traf fic network. However, the underlying 

mechanism by which such global information is modeled and 

extracted remains insufficiently investigated. Whether global 

information must be extracted by high -degree -of -freedom 

adaptive attention or can be captured by a  simple global ag-

gregation operator remains unclear. For this purpose, we de-

sign a controlled ablation framework that replaces only the 

spatial mixing module to test attention -based global interac-

tion. Across six traffic benchmarks, uniform full -range mix-

in g and standard spatial attention each achieve lower MAE 

on three datasets, with only a 0.14% difference in mean MAE, 

while the former reduces node -scale spatial mixing complex-

ity from O(N² ) to O(N). Mechanism analysis further decom-

poses spatial attention i nto a row -uniform global background 

and a non -uniform residual. The residual shows dataset -de-

pendent marginal value, suggesting that spatial attention 

should be justified by stable gains beyond a row -uniform 

global background.  The corresponding source code is pub-

licly available at : https://github.com/uuesti/U -Trans 

# 1 Introduction 

Traffic forecasting predicts future traffic states from his-

torical observations collected by road sensors or spatial 

grids. In addition to short -term inertia and daily or weekly 

periodicity, traffic systems exhibit local propagation, re-

gional co -movement,  and network -wide demand or conges-

tion patterns. In this work, global spatial information specif-

ically refers to the representations acquired by each individ-

ual node through interactions with all nodes in the traffic 

network, thereby capturing network -level  dependencies and 

shared traffic contexts beyond local neighborhoods. Recent 

traffic forecasting models commonly extract such broad 

spatial information through adaptive graphs, fully connected 

relations, spatial attention, dynamic long -range attention, or 

context broadcasting (Bai et al., 2020; Y. Fang et al., 2025; 

Jiang et al., 2023; Lin & Ren, 2024; Oreshkin et al., 2021; 

Zheng et al., 2020). 

However, the use of global spatial information does not 

specify the required extractor. The key issue is whether the 

gain comes from a shared network -level background or from 

adaptive pairwise node -to -node weights. This paper asks 

whether useful global spa tial information must be extracted 

by high -degree -of -freedom adaptive attention, or whether a 

simple global aggregation operator is often sufficient. 

Existing comparisons do not isolate this question. Graph 

propagation, adaptive adjacency, graph learning, and spatial 

attention have all improved traffic forecasting (Bai et al., 

2020; Jiang et al., 2023; Li et al., 2017; Z. Wu et al., 2019; 

Z. Wu et al.,  2020; Yu et al., 2017; Zheng et al., 2020), but 

these models often change representation, temporal encod-

ing, prediction head, and training protocol together. Strong 

representation -centric and simple baselines further show 

that non -spatial factors can domin ate model performance (H. 

Liu et al., 2023; X. Liu et al., 2023; Shao, Zhang, Wang, et 

al., 2022; Z. Wang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). 

A closer but still under -examined case is global mean or 

low -degree -of -freedom global mixing. Recent traffic fore-

casting Transformers have introduced spatio -temporal con-

text broadcasting to redistribute attention scores, making 

mean -style global background  injection relevant to traffic 

forecasting (Lin & Ren, 2024). Yet such components are 

embedded in larger architectures and are not evaluated as 

isolated spatial mixers. Outside traffic forecasting, query -in-

dependent global context, uniform attention, Fouri er token 

mixing, and other simple mixers suggest that low -degree -of -

freedom global information transfer can be surprisingly ef-

fective (Cao et al., 2019; Hyeon -Woo et al., 2023; Lee -

Thorp et al., 2022). Thus, global mean broadcasting should 

be treated as a  serious baseline rather than a trivial ablation. 

To address this identification problem, we first use trans-

fer entropy (TE) as a preliminary information -source check, 

then use prediction ablations to test whether global mixing †Corresponding author: zhaozhang@buaa.edu.cn lowers forecasting error, and propose a hypothesized model 

that decomposes attention into the row mean and residual to 

explain the experimental findings. 

The contributions are: 

(1) We build a controlled framework and introduce U -

Trans, a row -uniform full -range mixer based on node -mean 

broadcasting. 

(2) Through TE checks, controlled ablations, efficiency 

analysis, and public -baseline comparison, we show that 

global mean broadcasting is a strong global spatial baseline. 

(3) We propose a residual decomposition model and find 

that attention residuals are strongly dataset -dependent, caus-

ing standard attention to be not necessarily better than global 

mean. 

# 2 Related Work 

2.1 Spatial Modeling and Simple Baselines in Traf-

fic Forecasting 

Traffic forecasting spatial modules range from fixed to-

pology propagation to flexible cross -node interaction. Early 

models use road networks, diffusion, graph convolutions, or 

graph attention (Guo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2017; Yu et al., 

2017). Adaptive a nd dynamic variants use node embeddings, 

graph learning, continuous dynamics, and dynamic spatio -

temporal graphs (Bai et al., 2020; Choi et al., 2022; Z. Fang 

et al., 2021; Shao, Zhang, Wei, et al., 2022; Z. Wu et al., 

2019; Z. Wu et al., 2020). 

Another line questions whether complex spatial modules 

are always necessary. Spatial -temporal identity, adaptive 

embeddings, MLP predictors, linear models, and light-

weight baselines remain competitive when representation 

and training are strong (H. Chen et  al., 2025; H. Liu et al., 

2023; X. Liu et al., 2023; Shao, Zhang, Wang, et al., 2022; 

Z. Wang et al., 2023; Zeng et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). 

2.2 Global Context and Low -Degree -of -Freedom 

Mixing 

Global and long -range context is modeled through global 

spatial -temporal networks, fully connected gates, multi -at-

tention, spatial Transformers, dynamic long -range attention, 

spectral attention, scalable spatial patching, and context 

broadcasting (S. Fang  et al., 2019; Y. Fang et al., 2025; Jiang 

et al., 2023; Lin & Ren, 2024; Oreshkin et al., 2021; Xu et 

al., 2020; Zheng et al., 2020). Large -scale work exposes 

dense -interaction cost (Han et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2026; B. 

Wang et al., 2024). 

Low -degree -of -freedom mixing gives a complementary 

view. Non -local attention analysis, query -independent 

global context, fixed graph smoothing, MLP token mixing, 

Fourier mixing, and uniform attention show that global con-

text can sometimes be carried by sim ple, nearly uniform op-

erators rather than learned pairwise weights (Cao et al., 2019; 

Hyeon -Woo et al., 2023; Lee -Thorp et al., 2022; Tolstikhin 

et al., 2021; X. Wang et al., 2018; F. Wu et al., 2019).

4.2 Preliminary Check: Is Global Traffic Infor-

mation Useful? 

Before the prediction ablations, we use transfer entropy 

(TE) as a preliminary information -source check. TE tests 

whether a target -excluded fully connected source (FC) adds 

information about target future flow beyond target history, 

periodicity, and conges tion state. We report corrected TE by 

subtracting the permutation -null mean from observed TE. 

Upstream -downstream k -hop local sources (UD1, UD3, 

UD5, and UD10) are compared with the target -excluded 

fully connected source (FC); Appendix C gives the full est i-

mation and sensitivity protocol. 

Figure 2: Corrected TE for spatial -source ranges. 

As shown in Figure 2, corrected TE increases from UD1 

to UD10, and FC exceeds UD5 on PEMS03, PEMS04, and 

PEMS07 by 0.0450, 0.0360, and 0.0363, respectively .

This experiment shows, from the TE perspective, that 

global information is worth extracting and that the fully con-

nected source can carry more information than upstream -

downstream local neighborhoods.

Adaptive Attention? 

The prediction ablations next test whether a uniform 

full -range mixer can convert the FC information identified 

above into lower forecasting error. The controlled compari-

son includes No -SAB, I -Trans, UD -Trans, U -Trans, and S -

Trans, with MAE results shown i n Figure 4 and complete 

metrics reported in Appendix D.

U-Trans outperforms No -SAB on all six datasets.  More 

importantly, I -Trans and U -Trans share the same spatial -

block scaffold and differ only in node aggregation: I -Trans 

uses identity aggregation, whereas U -Trans broadcasts the 

full -range node mean. U -Trans achieves lower MAE than I -

Trans on five of the  six datasets, indicating that row -uniform

row -uniform baseline.  Section 5 reports the same pattern for

# 6 Conclusion 

TE checks and prediction ablations show that global traf-

fic states provide useful predictive signals. U -Trans, a row -

uniform global mean broadcasting operator, improves over 

no spatial mixing and stays close to or better than S -Trans 

across road -sensor and  grid -demand benchmarks while re-

ducing node -scale mixing cost. 

Mechanism analysis explains this result by separating S -

Trans into a row -uniform global background and a non -uni-

form residual. The residual is dataset -dependent: it can help, 

hurt, or have little effect. Spatial Transformer modules 

should therefore be eval uated against global mean broad-

casting and justified by stable gains beyond this uniform 

background. U F
